The **Geely EX2** is a fully electric city car designed for urban and suburban mobility. With a real-world range between **280 and 320 kilometres** in mixed driving, this compact electric vehicle from the Geely group competes directly with established players in the B-segment electric market. But how does the gap between official WLTP figures and real-world performance get explained? Which factors make range vary? And which battery should you choose based on your needs? We offer a complete breakdown of the Geely EX2's range.

> **At a glance:**
>
> - The Geely EX2's WLTP range varies from 225 km (35.4 kWh battery) to 325 km (47 kWh battery)
> - In real-world usage, you will travel between 280 and 320 km depending on driving conditions
> - DC charging allows you to go from 30 to 80 percent in 21 to 25 minutes
> - Cold weather, motorway driving, and heating significantly reduce range
> - Compared to rivals Dacia Spring and Leapmotor T03, the Geely EX2 offers good value for money

## WLTP range: official figures {#wltp-range}

The **Geely EX2** is equipped with lithium iron phosphate (LFP) batteries available in two capacities: 35.4 kWh for entry-level versions and 47 kWh for higher trims. This architecture guarantees enhanced durability and better thermal safety compared to NCA/NCM chemistry.

According to the WLTP cycle (Worldwide Harmonized Light-Duty Vehicle Test Procedure), the official range figures are as follows:

| Battery | Capacity | WLTP Range |
|---|---|---|
| Small battery | around 35.4 kWh | from around 225 km |
| Large battery | around 47 kWh | up to around 325 km |

The WLTP cycle, introduced in 2017, is more representative of real-world conditions than the older NEDC standard, but it remains a theoretical average. Versions fitted with the large battery reach the highest values, while the entry version with the small battery offers more modest range.

## Real-world range: the gap from WLTP {#real-world-range}

In real-world usage, early testers find the **Geely EX2's** range sits between 280 and 320 kilometres in mixed driving (city, road, motorway combined). The gap from WLTP values remains contained, which reflects good efficiency for an electric city car.

On purely urban routes, with low cruising speed and frequent braking, range tends to meet or even exceed WLTP promises, as energy recovery during braking is maximized. Conversely, on motorways at sustained speeds, range approaches the lower end of this bracket.

As L'Argus notes (L'Argus, 2026), the small gap between theoretical and real figures shows that Geely has calibrated its test cycles reliably. This transparency is an asset for buyers hesitant about commercial promises in the electric vehicle market.

## NEDC and CLTC cycles: more optimistic {#nedc-cltc-cycles}

Before WLTP, the Geely EX2 was first homologated using the CLTC cycle (China Light-Duty Vehicle Test Cycle) in Asian markets, with considerably more generous figures: up to 395 km range for the best versions. These Asian cycles are known to be less stringent than WLTP.

In Europe, the NEDC (New European Driving Cycle), now obsolete, was also more optimistic. It is important not to confuse these older cycles with current WLTP values. If you encounter advertisements claiming range exceeding 350 km for the Geely EX2, they refer to CLTC measurements, not current European standards. WLTP remains the only reliable indicator for comparing European vehicles.

## Factors affecting range {#factors-range}

The real-world range of an electric vehicle like the **Geely EX2** is influenced by multiple parameters that no test cycle can fully integrate.

**Winter cold** is the primary range killer: once temperatures drop below 10 degrees Celsius, LFP batteries see their available capacity reduced, and activating cabin heating consumes enormous energy. In strict winter conditions, expect a 20 to 30 percent reduction in range.

**Cruising speed** on motorways increases aerodynamic drag exponentially. A journey at 130 km/h consumes significantly more than at 90 km/h. At sustained high speed, real-world range can drop by 15 to 25 percent.

**Driving style** plays a decisive role: aggressive driving with frequent acceleration and speed variations drains the battery much faster than smooth, anticipatory driving. Conversely, maximizing energy recovery during braking delivers substantial gains.

**Vehicle payload** is not neutral. Carrying four passengers and luggage increases mass and therefore consumption. An additional 100 kg load can reduce range by 3 to 5 percent.

**Terrain and slopes** also matter: driving in mountains demands more energy than level driving. Hilly routes reduce range by 10 to 15 percent compared to flat terrain.

## Charging and charge times {#charging}

The **Geely EX2** accepts two distinct charging modes, with very different impacts on downtime.

**Alternating current (AC) charging**: Using an onboard 7.4 kW charger compatible with reinforced domestic outlets or public AC chargers, a full charge of the 47 kWh battery requires approximately 5 hours. For the 35.4 kWh battery, expect 4 hours. This is ideal for overnight home or workplace charging without urgency.

**Direct current (DC) charging**: Rapid DC charging stations deliver power up to 80 kW on the Geely EX2. The gain is spectacular: from 30 to 80 percent charge in just 21 to 25 minutes. Beyond 80 percent, charging speed slows to preserve LFP battery health. This capability makes the Geely EX2 a strong candidate for long-distance trips or multiple journeys throughout the day.

Rapid DC charging times make the Geely EX2 very practical daily. An owner with access to a DC charger can theoretically complete multiple short trips during the day without absolute need for a full overnight charge.

## Comparison with rivals {#rivals-comparison}

In the affordable compact electric car segment, the **Geely EX2** faces three main rivals: the Dacia Spring, BYD Dolphin Surf, and Leapmotor T03.

The **Dacia Spring**, the segment pioneer, offers WLTP range of 210 to 305 km depending on versions, with a smaller NMC chemistry battery. The Geely EX2 slightly edges ahead on top-end range, with a safer and more durable LFP battery.

The **BYD Dolphin Surf**, less common in France but formidable on range, offers variants with 44.9 kWh LFP batteries delivering up to 305 km WLTP. Real-world range measured is comparable to the Geely EX2.

The **Leapmotor T03**, another Chinese newcomer sold in Europe, offers WLTP range of 305 to 405 km on best versions with 49 kWh battery. It surpasses the Geely EX2 on raw capacity, but at a higher price.

For range per euro, the Geely EX2 offers good value. It targets buyers seeking a reliable compact electric with LFP battery and sufficient range for daily urban and suburban journeys. For those considering the electric segment with [Geely's electric SUV, the E5, available at Como](https://como.fr/pages/geely-e5), the Geely EX2 is an excellent showcase of the group's electric expertise.

With a real-world range of 280 to 320 km, two charging options (AC and DC), and an LFP battery renowned for durability, the Geely EX2 delivers on its promises.
