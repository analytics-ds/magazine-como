Searching for a **Geely Coolray review** quickly leads to one fact: this Chinese manufacturer's urban SUV is not sold in France, so no French test can speak to it first-hand. Reviews do exist, though, plenty of them, across the markets where the model has been sold since 2018. This article gathers reviews from owners and international testers, market by market, to build an honest picture of its strengths and limits, before looking at what this means in practice for a French buyer tempted by a used unit.

## In brief

> - The **Geely Coolray** is not sold in France: available reviews come from export markets (Maghreb, Philippines, South Africa) and international forums
> - Design and equipment seen as generous for the segment, with a large central screen and neat finish on higher trims
> - The 1.5-turbo engine is well liked for its pickup, the DCT gearbox is sometimes criticised at low speed
> - Real-world urban fuel consumption is often cited as above homologated figures
> - Reliability is seen as decent, backed by mechanics co-developed with Volvo and several years of commercial track record
> - In France, only the imported used-car route exists, priced between 19,990 and 23,000 euros

## A review of an SUV absent from French dealerships {#review-coolray}

Before getting into the details of the reviews, one clarification matters: the **Geely Coolray** has never featured in the Geely Auto France catalogue, which built its French range around electrified vehicles (EX2, Starray EM-i, E5). Our [full Geely Coolray specifications](/en/blog/geely-coolray/) cover its commercial career, which started in the Philippines in 2019 before reaching the Maghreb and then South Africa.

This lack of official distribution changes the nature of the exercise. A review of this vehicle cannot rely on a test carried out in France, since no homologated unit is available to the local press. It does, however, rely on abundant material: owner feedback from the markets where it is sold, tests from local specialist press (Morocco, Ukraine, Southeast Asia), and testimonials published on social media and international car forums.

This is the material this article gathers and puts into perspective, supplemented by the data already covered in our article on [Geely Coolray pricing](/en/blog/geely-coolray-price/) for readers considering a used unit in France.

## Design and exterior presentation {#design}

Reviews of the **Coolray**'s design are unanimously positive, even in markets where compact SUV competition is dense. The crossover shows a dynamic silhouette, with automatic LED headlights, a prominent rear spoiler and 17- or 18-inch wheels depending on trim. A Moroccan tester from Autonews.ma notes a compact footprint and a tight turning circle, qualities that come up regularly in reviews published across North African markets.

The styling update rolled out from 2022 (Binyue Cool variant) modernised the bumpers and lighting, a point praised in the most recent reviews compared with the first 2018-2019 model years. On Sportline trims, the two-tone red and black upholstery and customisable ambient lighting reinforce an interior presentation seen as good value for the segment.

A review published on DD Tuning sums up the consensus well: an updated design, paired with a powerful engine and a rich set of features, makes it an attractive proposition on its market. The more critical reviews mostly regret a style that stays conventional in its broad lines, without a strong break from segment B norms.

The choice of colours and two-tone combinations also comes up regularly in the most detailed reviews, particularly on Sportline trims that pair a dark body with contrasting wheel elements. This attention to exterior presentation partly explains why the Coolray has long ranked among the best-selling Chinese models in its home markets, alongside other [Chinese SUVs](/en/blog/chinese-suv/) that bet on the same strategy of bold styling at a contained price.

## Cabin: equipment and onboard technology {#interior-technology}

This is one of the points most frequently cited in positive reviews of the **Geely Coolray**. The interior runs a Flyme Auto digital interface, with a large central screen (up to 14.6 inches on the Sportline trim) and a resolution seen as high for the category. A tester also notes keyless entry, wireless charging and a power tailgate on the best-equipped versions, attributes rare on an urban SUV at this price point.

Standard equipment exceeds the segment average in several markets: automatic climate control, heated front seats and steering wheel, cruise control, power mirrors and windows, reversing camera. These are precisely the points reviews most often highlight when comparing the Coolray to Western rivals in the same segment.

Reviews found on social media also stress the perceived comfort on board: materials seen as neat on higher trims, controls judged intuitive, and decent legroom thanks to a 2,600-millimetre wheelbase. A review posted on a Philippine car forum sums it up: pleasant to drive, almost effortless, 360-degree camera, neat interior. The points of concern mostly concern entry-level trims, where some comfort features (heated seats, ambient lighting) remain reserved for higher versions.

## On the road: engine, DCT gearbox and handling {#driving}

On the road, reviews clearly distinguish between the two main engines offered depending on the market. The **1.5-turbo engine, delivering 177 to 181 hp**, paired with a 7-speed dual-clutch DCT gearbox, gathers most of the positive feedback on pickup and everyday driveability. The 136 hp 1.0-turbo, more modest, stays more confined to urban use according to reviews from markets where both engines coexist.

| Criterion | Summarised review |
|---|---|
| 1.5-turbo engine | Strong pickup, well rated by testers |
| 7-speed DCT gearbox | Smooth on the road, less responsive at low speed |
| Chassis and handling | Stable in fast corners, chassis seen as rigid |
| Real-world consumption | Often cited above homologated figures in town |
| Ride comfort | Seen as decent for an urban SUV of this size |

A review posted on a Facebook group dedicated to the model describes a chassis that "stays flat and unfazed" in fast corners, a point echoed across several independent reviews. The DCT gearbox draws more mixed reviews: praised for its smoothness on the road, it is more often criticised in urban use at low speed, a classic flaw of this transmission architecture also found on other Geely group models, as our [Geely EX2 review](/en/blog/geely-ex2-review/) notes regarding its electric powertrain.

Real-world consumption from the turbo engine, higher than homologated figures in urban use, also comes up in several reviews, without being a deal-breaker for the owners interviewed.

Ride comfort draws more mixed reviews depending on road surface: on degraded roads, the Coolray's suspension is described as firm without being uncomfortable, a fair compromise for an urban SUV rather than an off-road-oriented vehicle. Reviews published in African markets, where secondary road networks can be demanding, stress sufficient ground clearance to absorb common irregularities without scraping the underbody.

## Fuel consumption and running costs according to reviews {#consumption}

One point recurs with notable regularity in **Coolray** owner reviews: the gap between homologated and real-world consumption, particularly marked in urban use with frequent stops and restarts. The 1.5-turbo engine, more generous in torque than the entry-level 1.0-turbo, pays for this extra performance with higher consumption on short trips.

The most thorough reviews on the subject recommend favouring regular highway use to get closer to manufacturer-stated figures, advice valid for most turbo petrol engines of this size. On the maintenance side, the absence of a Geely network in France remains the main concern for any buyer of an imported unit: no manufacturer service plan covers a vehicle that is neither homologated nor officially distributed in the country.

## Safety and driving aids {#safety}

Reviews on safety equipment remain scarcer, in the absence of independent tests by European bodies such as Euro NCAP: the Coolray has never been submitted to these tests, not being sold in Western Europe. In its selling markets, standard safety equipment includes ABS, electronic stability control (ESP), front and side airbags, and a tyre pressure monitoring system.

Higher trims add a reversing camera, or even a 360-degree camera depending on the market, equipment that several owner reviews explicitly cite as reassuring for city manoeuvring. More advanced driving aids (automatic emergency braking, lane keeping) are unevenly present depending on version and market, which explains the relative scarcity of reviews on this chapter compared with interior or design.

## What owners say {#owner-reviews}

Beyond professional tests, owner reviews published on forums and social media paint a consistent picture of the vehicle in daily use.

### Strengths cited in reviews

- Equipment-to-price ratio seen as very favourable in commercial markets
- Comfortable urban driving, steering seen as light and manoeuvrable
- Finish and onboard technology level above the segment average
- Mechanical reliability seen as satisfactory after several years on the market
- 330-litre boot, expandable to 1,038 litres, seen as practical for the category

### Points of concern raised in reviews

- Real-world consumption from the turbo engine above homologated figures in town
- DCT gearbox responsiveness could be better at low speed and while manoeuvring
- Comfort features unevenly spread across trims and markets
- No independent European homologation or safety tests
- Equipment variability across model years, a point of caution for used buyers

A testimonial found on a vehicle export site illustrates this general tone well: the vehicle and supplier service are rated 5 out of 5, against 4 out of 5 for on-time delivery, a gap the customer considered minor given their overall satisfaction.

## Geely Coolray and Volvo: reliability seen through reviews {#reliability}

Reliability is a question that comes up systematically in reviews of a Chinese model, and the **Coolray** has an argument here that few recent rivals can claim: its age. Sold under this name since 2019 for export (and since 2018 in China as the Binyue), it has accumulated several years of usage feedback across markets as varied as the Philippines, South Africa and the Maghreb.

Its 1.5-turbo mechanics stem from the **technical cooperation between Geely and Volvo**, a link our article on [Geely Coolray specifications](/en/blog/geely-coolray/) covers at greater length, and which weighs favourably in the technical reviews consulted. The model is also sold under other badges (Proton X50 in Malaysia, BelGee X50 in Eastern Europe), a wide distribution that contributes to the perceived industrial maturity noted by owners.

The most critical reviews on reliability point to two recurring elements: wear of the dual-clutch gearbox over the long term, a trait common to this type of transmission, and a variability in perceived quality between the first 2018-2019 model years and the more recent restyled versions. No major structural flaw emerges, however, from the reviews gathered for this article.

## Should you buy a used Geely Coolray in France? {#used-france}

The positive reviews gathered from export markets should not obscure a reality specific to the French market: no **Coolray** unit there benefits from a manufacturer warranty, official homologation or a dedicated parts network. The rare units in circulation come from individual imports, priced between 19,990 and 23,000 euros depending on mileage and model year, as detailed in our article on [Geely Coolray pricing](/en/blog/geely-coolray-price/).

A French buyer must therefore weigh the positive reviews gathered abroad against these local constraints: no manufacturer follow-up, uncertain parts supply, and a resale hard to value without an official price guide. ## Verdict: does the Geely Coolray live up to its positive reviews? {#verdict}

Reviews gathered across commercial markets confirm a consistent model: neat design, a well-equipped cabin for its price point, a well-liked turbo engine despite somewhat high real-world consumption, and mechanical reliability seen as decent after several years of track record. The most frequently cited concerns, DCT gearbox responsiveness at low speed and the lack of European homologation, do not fundamentally undermine this broadly positive assessment.

The French question remains: without official distribution, these positive reviews do not translate directly into a worry-free purchase on the French market. For anyone looking for a similarly positioned Geely model, warrantied and available at a dealership, the alternatives mentioned above remain the more sensible choice.
