How much does an oil change cost in 2026? The answer mainly depends on the type of engine, the oil quality chosen and where the job gets done: from 70 euros for a petrol city car at a service centre up to 450 euros for a premium manufacturer package. This guide breaks down the average oil change price by vehicle profile, what a package actually includes, the gap between engine types and the practical levers to cut your maintenance bill.

## How much does an oil change cost on average in 2026?

The cost of an oil change always breaks down into two items: the price of the engine oil and the price of labour, plus the oil filter. For a petrol city car, the average budget sits between 70 and 130 euros. For a mid-range saloon or SUV, the range climbs to 100 to 180 euros, mainly because the required oil volume is higher and premium synthetic oil is almost systematic on these recent models.

On a premium vehicle such as a Mercedes-Benz, the manufacturer oil change package (Service A or Service B) generally costs between 250 and 450 euros. This gap is explained by a more demanding oil specification (often an MB 229.5 or 229.71 standard), a longer multipoint check and genuine parts billed at manufacturer rates. Labour alone accounts for 20 to 45 euros for 30 to 45 minutes of work, a duration that increases slightly on engines with more complex access.

| Vehicle type | Average oil change price | Oil used |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol city car | €70 to €130 | Semi-synthetic or synthetic 5W30 |
| Petrol saloon / SUV | €100 to €180 | Synthetic 5W30 or 0W20 |
| Diesel saloon / SUV | €120 to €220 | Low-ash synthetic |
| Premium vehicle (manufacturer package) | €250 to €450 | Manufacturer-spec synthetic |

These figures remain averages observed on the French market in 2026: an exact quote always depends on the precise model, its mileage and the ongoing promotions offered by service networks.

## What is included in a full oil change package?

A full oil change package is not limited to draining and refilling the oil pan. It systematically includes replacing the oil filter, whose condition directly affects the cleanliness of the newly injected oil. Most centres add a check of fluid levels: coolant, brake fluid, windscreen washer fluid, with top-ups included in the base package.

A visual check of safety points almost always comes with the job: brake pad wear, tyre pressure and condition, lights working properly. This free diagnostic helps anticipate other maintenance items, such as [changing the timing belt](/en/blog/when-to-change-timing-belt/), before a breakdown occurs. On the other hand, replacing the air filter, cabin filter or wiper blades is generally billed as an extra, even if some premium packages bundle them into a single price.

Asking for a detailed quote before the job lets you check what is actually included: a package advertised at a low price sometimes omits the oil filter or limits the oil volume supplied, with an extra charge added once the car is already on the lift.

## Petrol or diesel: what is the price gap for an oil change?

The choice between [diesel or petrol](/en/blog/diesel-or-petrol/) directly affects annual maintenance costs, and the oil change is no exception. A diesel engine requires 20 to 40 euros more on average than an equivalent petrol engine for the same job. Two main reasons explain this gap: the oil volume to be replaced is often higher on a diesel, and recent engines fitted with a particulate filter require low-ash oil (C2 or C3 standard), which is pricier than standard oil.

Some diesel models also require the diesel filter to be replaced at the same interval as the oil change, an item that adds 30 to 60 euros to the final bill. Conversely, a large-displacement petrol engine, notably a V6 or a hybrid engine with a large-capacity sump, can get close to diesel pricing. The gap therefore narrows on premium segments, where oil volume weighs more than fuel type in the final calculation.

## Where to get an oil change: garage, service centre or Mercedes-Benz dealer?

Where the job gets done makes the final price vary as much as the vehicle type. A general service centre offers the lowest rates, between 70 and 150 euros for a common city car or saloon, with appointments available quickly and often without booking at most chains. An independent garage sits on a similar range, 80 to 160 euros, with the advantage of personalised follow-up of the vehicle over time.

A dealership charges higher rates, but with guarantees specific to the manufacturer network: genuine parts, oil at the engine's exact specification, a digital service book update recognised at resale. For a vehicle still under warranty, sticking to this route is often the condition for keeping contractual cover in the event of an engine failure. The [maintenance and servicing packages offered by Como](https://como.fr/contrats-entretien) precisely help smooth out this cost over time, with a fixed rate covering several consecutive oil changes on the Mercedes-Benz, smart and Lexus vehicles of the network.

Comparing several quotes before booking a slot remains the most effective habit to avoid paying more than an equivalent service elsewhere, for identical part quality.

## How often should you change your car's oil?

The recommended oil change frequency depends both on the manufacturer and the type of oil used. On most recent vehicles, the interval sits between 15,000 and 30,000 kilometres, with a systematic one-year cap even for low annual mileage. This cap exists because engine oil also degrades over time, independently of use, due to oxidation and condensation.

Mostly urban use, with short trips and frequent cold starts, wears out oil faster than regular motorway use: it is then better to stick to the shortest interval recommended by the manufacturer. Sticking to this schedule also affects the outcome of the [vehicle inspection](/en/blog/when-to-get-vehicle-inspection/), since a poorly maintained engine carries a higher risk of failure during mandatory checks.

The vehicle's digital service book, or failing that the maintenance indicator shown on the dashboard, remains the most reliable reference for knowing the exact date of the next oil change, rather than a rough mileage estimate.

## How to reduce the cost of your oil change?

Comparing several quotes remains the most direct lever to cut the bill: the gap between two service centres for an identical job can reach 30 to 50 euros on a city car. Grouping the oil change with other maintenance jobs planned at the same time, such as a fluid level check or a [Crit'Air sticker](/en/blog/crit-air-sticker/) check before driving into a low-emission zone, also limits the number of workshop visits.

Taking out a manufacturer service contract, when offered, smooths the cost over several years rather than facing a higher one-off bill at each visit. Finally, checking the oil level yourself between two oil changes helps anticipate a top-up at a lower cost rather than an emergency job, which always includes extra labour charges.
