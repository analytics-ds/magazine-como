Asking whether **Volvo was bought by the Chinese** means looking into a precise episode of automotive industrial history: a dated acquisition, an identified buyer, and a known price. The short answer is one sentence, the Chinese carmaker Geely has owned Volvo Cars since 2010, but the details of the process and what it has actually changed for the Swedish brand deserve a clear answer, alongside our broader piece on whether [Volvo is a Chinese brand](/en/blog/is-volvo-chinese/).

## Which company bought Volvo, and when

The acquisition of Volvo Cars by the Chinese group Geely Holding was finalized in **2010**, for around 1.8 billion dollars. The deal was struck with **Ford Motor Company**, which had owned the Swedish brand since 1999 within its Premier Automotive Group division, alongside Jaguar and Land Rover during the same period.

That figure still makes this deal one of the largest acquisitions ever made abroad by a Chinese carmaker at the time, a benchmark that durably shaped how Western markets perceived Geely.

## How the Volvo takeover by Geely unfolded

Negotiations began in 2009, in a particular context for Ford: weakened by the 2008 financial crisis, the American group had started refocusing its strategy on its North American brands and sought to sell off the European premium brands it had acquired a few years earlier. Volvo, seen as costly to develop on its own against better-resourced German rivals, was no longer considered strategic by Ford.

Geely positioned itself as the buyer with an argument that reassured Swedish authorities and unions: preserving the brand's operational autonomy, keeping the headquarters in Gothenburg, and guaranteeing continued industrial investment on site. That commitment, made at signing, has largely been honored fifteen years later, which explains why Volvo is still perceived as a Swedish company despite its Chinese ownership.

## Volvo Cars and Volvo Group: two clearly separate companies

A common confusion deserves clearing up: Geely's acquisition only covered **Volvo Cars**, the passenger car division. **Volvo Group**, which builds heavy trucks, buses and construction equipment under the Volvo name, has been a fully independent company since 1999, the year Ford had already bought only the passenger car branch from the original Swedish group.

Both entities share the same name today through a simple brand licensing agreement, but have no shared ownership, management or strategy. Volvo Group remains listed on the Stockholm Stock Exchange and therefore does not belong to Geely, contrary to a misconception that regularly circulates in France.

## Who is Geely, Volvo's Chinese owner

**Geely Holding Group** is a private industrial conglomerate founded in 1986 by entrepreneur Li Shufu, based in Hangzhou. Starting out making refrigerators and then motorcycles before entering the car industry in the mid-1990s, the group has since built a particularly broad portfolio of Western and Chinese brands.

Besides Volvo Cars, Geely Holding also owns **Polestar**, the Swedish fully electric brand born from a collaboration with Volvo, a stake in British carmaker **Lotus**, a majority stake in Malaysian manufacturer **Proton**, and **Zeekr**, its own premium electric brand whose arrival in France is covered in our comparison of [Chinese electric cars](/en/blog/chinese-electric-cars-france/) on the market. Li Shufu also personally holds a stake in Mercedes-Benz Group, with no operational link to the management of the German brand.

## What has changed at Volvo since the Chinese takeover

Under Geely's ownership, Volvo has invested heavily in electrification, developing the SEA technical platform shared across several of the group's brands, which underpins the EX30 among others. This shared investment has allowed costs to be spread in a way Volvo likely could not have financed alone under Ford, while keeping its own engineering teams in Gothenburg.

China has also become, over the decade following the takeover, one of the brand's top markets by volume, alongside the United States and Europe. Volvo Cars also carried out a partial listing on the Stockholm Stock Exchange in 2021, with Geely Holding retaining a majority stake, a move that did not change the brand's governance or design autonomy. This path mirrors that of other [Chinese car brands](/en/blog/chinese-car-brands/) that favor acquiring European know-how over building entirely in-house.

## What this takeover means for a buyer in France

For a driver comparing Volvo with other premium brands, this Chinese ownership has in practice no direct impact on the buying experience: the dealer network, warranty standards and after-sales service are still organized the same way as before 2010. The Como group, a Mercedes-Benz dealer in the Paris region, also offers new Geely models, through the [Como group's new Geely offers](https://como.fr/pages/offres-neufs-geely), a concrete way to compare a premium European offer and a recent Chinese one at the same dealership.

This coexistence between Chinese capital and an established distribution network in France illustrates a broader market trend, already covered in our overview of [Chinese cars in France](/en/blog/chinese-cars-france/), where several carmakers follow the same gradual entry strategy.

<details>
<summary>Is Volvo a Chinese brand?</summary>

Both, depending on the angle. Volvo Cars remains Swedish on the operational side, with its headquarters in Gothenburg and a mostly Scandinavian leadership, but its capital has belonged 100% to the Chinese group Geely Holding since 2010.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Does Geely also own Volvo Trucks?</summary>

No. Volvo Trucks, which builds heavy trucks, buses and construction equipment, belongs to the AB Volvo group, an entity entirely separate from Volvo Cars since 1999. Geely's 2010 acquisition only covered the passenger car division.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Where are Volvo cars manufactured today?</summary>

Production is still split between the historic Torslanda plant in Sweden and the Ghent plant in Belgium, complemented since the takeover by Chinese sites in Chengdu and Daqing, part of whose output is exported to Europe.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is Volvo's country of origin?</summary>

Volvo is a brand founded in Gothenburg, Sweden, in 1927. Its ownership has changed several times over the years, passing through Ford between 1999 and 2010 before the full takeover by the Chinese group Geely Holding.

</details>
