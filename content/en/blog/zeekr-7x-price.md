The **price of the Zeekr 7X** starts at **€52,990** in France for the entry-level version, and climbs to around €60,000 for the most powerful all-wheel drive version. From the pricing grid by version to monthly lease payments and the real cost versus the electric competition, here is the full breakdown of the budget to plan for this electric SUV from the Geely group.

## How much does the Zeekr 7X cost in France? The full pricing grid

The **Zeekr 7X** has been sold in France since April 2026 in three configurations, each with its own price positioning:

| Version | Battery | WLTP Range | Power | Indicative Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **7X Rear-Wheel Drive** | 75 kWh | 480 km | 421 hp | €52,990 |
| **7X Long Range** | 100 kWh | 615 km | 421 hp | around €57,000 |
| **7X AWD Performance** | 100 kWh | 543 km | 646 hp | around €60,000 |

The gap between the entry version and the all-wheel drive version exceeds €7,000, justified as much by the larger battery as by the doubling of power on the all-wheel drive version. None of these prices include any purchase incentive: the ecological bonus remains closed to vehicles manufactured outside the European Union, which applies to the entire **Zeekr** lineup.

### Price by version: rear-wheel drive, Long Range and all-wheel drive AWD

The **Rear-Wheel Drive 75 kWh** version is the logical entry point for a budget-conscious buyer: at €52,990, it stays under the symbolic €55,000 mark while offering 480 km of certified range, enough for most daily use and occasional long trips.

The **Long Range** version, with its battery increased to 100 kWh, adds around €4,000 to the price but pushes range up to 615 km, a proportionally larger gain than the price increase. It is the configuration that offers the best budget-to-range compromise in the whole lineup.

The **AWD Performance** version, with all-wheel drive and 646 combined hp, targets a buyer chasing performance rather than maximum range: its battery, identical to the Long Range, only delivers 543 km WLTP, the extra power and grip weighing on consumption.

## Financing the Zeekr 7X: LOA, LLD and the ecological bonus

As with the rest of the **Zeekr** lineup, buying the 7X comes without any public incentive: since the vehicle is assembled in China, it is excluded from the French ecological bonus scale regardless of its actual carbon footprint. This exclusion weighs more heavily on entry-level versions, where the bonus would have represented a significant share of the budget, than on a model priced above €50,000.

Based on a 48-month, 40,000-kilometre financing plan, observed monthly payments run approximately as follows:

- **€590 to €650 per month** for the Rear-Wheel Drive 75 kWh version
- **€640 to €700 per month** for the Long Range version
- **€680 to €750 per month** for the AWD Performance version

These amounts vary depending on the down payment and generally do not include insurance or maintenance. For business use, VAT remains recoverable on LOA or LLD lease payments under the usual conditions applicable to company vehicles, a lever that meaningfully reduces the real cost for a business or a self-employed buyer. The 10-year manufacturer warranty, included in the catalogue price, partly limits the financial risk over the ownership period.

## The real cost over 4 years: cash purchase, LOA or LLD

Beyond the catalogue price, the choice of financing method meaningfully changes the real cost of the **Zeekr 7X** over time. With a cash purchase, the budget is limited to the purchase price minus a resale value that remains uncertain for a brand that has only recently arrived on the French market, for lack of sufficient track record on used values.

With LLD, the monthly budget already factors in the depreciation estimated by the leasing company, which secures the budget but rules out any resale at the end, since the vehicle is returned when the contract ends. LOA, a middle ground, keeps a purchase option that lets a buyer keep the vehicle if its residual value turns out to be attractive after the 48 months. For a model whose depreciation track record has yet to be written, LLD limits the risk a buyer takes on resale value, at the cost of a generally slightly higher monthly payment than LOA for an equivalent down payment.
