**Zeekr** is the premium electric brand of the Chinese group Geely, which arrived in France in April 2026 with a range of four to five models spanning an urban SUV to a large family shooting brake. Less known in Europe than Tesla or German brands, it draws on one of the world's largest automotive groups, already the owner of Volvo and Polestar. This guide covers the brand's origins, its full range, its positioning against the competition, and useful pointers before considering a purchase.

## Zeekr, the premium electric brand of the Geely group

Founded in March 2021, **Zeekr** is the high-end, fully electric division of Geely Holding, one of China's largest automotive groups. The manufacturer passed the 500,000 vehicles produced mark in June 2025, a rapid growth pace for such a young brand.

All Zeekr models are built on the modular **SEA** platform (Sustainable Experience Architecture), an electric architecture designed by Geely and shared with other brands in the group, including Volvo. This technical kinship explains why some models, such as the [Zeekr 009](/en/blog/zeekr-009/) minivan, have a direct cousin at Volvo: the EM90 uses the same mechanical base with styling and tuning specific to the Swedish brand.

The Geely group also owns Lotus and a stake in smart alongside Mercedes-Benz, placing Zeekr within a broad industrial ecosystem, with shared platforms and components that reduce development costs.

## The Zeekr range: which electric models are available

Zeekr's European range is built around four main models, from the compact urban SUV to the large premium shooting brake. Each targets a different use case, with significant differences in price and range.

| Model | Type | Price from | WLTP range |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Zeekr X](/en/blog/zeekr-x/) | Compact urban SUV | €37,990 | up to 415 km |
| [Zeekr 7GT](/en/blog/zeekr-7gt/) | Sport wagon | €45,990 | up to 655 km |
| [Zeekr 7X](/en/blog/zeekr-7x/) | Family SUV | €52,990 | up to 615 km |
| [Zeekr 001](/en/blog/zeekr-001/) | Large premium wagon | €59,990 | up to 620 km |

The [Zeekr X](/en/blog/zeekr-x/) is the most accessible entry point in the range, designed for urban and suburban use. At the other end, the [Zeekr 001](/en/blog/zeekr-001/) takes on a more status-oriented positioning, close to large premium European saloons and wagons.

Zeekr also sells a luxury minivan, the [Zeekr 009](/en/blog/zeekr-009/), but this model remains reserved for the Chinese market and a few Asian markets: it is not offered in France. The brand is also working on new models, including a large 9X SUV mentioned in the trade press, with no confirmed European launch date so far.

Beyond price and size differences, the whole range shares a common technology base: fast DC charging, a large infotainment screen, level 2 driver-assistance systems and over-the-air updates. This consistency, inherited from the SEA platform, lets Zeekr offer features from the entry-level trim that other brands reserve for their top-of-range versions.

## Zeekr in France: launch, prices and dealer network

Zeekr officially opened order books in France on April 2, 2026, with first deliveries from May for the X and 7X models. The 001 and 7GT followed from July 2026. The brand chose to set up its first outlets in major cities, including Paris, Lyon and Aix-en-Provence, before gradually extending its network.

Full details on the launch timeline, per-version pricing and available showrooms are covered in our dedicated article on [Zeekr in France](/en/blog/zeekr-in-france/).

Like most Chinese manufacturers importing electric vehicles, Zeekr does not benefit from the French ecological bonus, which is reserved for vehicles produced under European environmental criteria. This lack of a bonus weighs on the sticker price against equivalent European competitors, a factor worth including in any budget comparison.

## Zeekr against the competition: Tesla, Volvo and premium brands

In the premium electric segment, Zeekr positions itself directly against Tesla, but also against the electric ranges of established European manufacturers. Its main strength is an aggressive equipment-to-price ratio: entry-level versions often include features reserved for options by the competition, such as 800-volt fast charging or large infotainment screens.

Against Volvo, its sister brand within the Geely group, Zeekr takes on a more technology-driven, sportier positioning, while Volvo keeps a more sober, family-oriented image despite a shared technical base. Buyers torn between a premium Chinese electric and an electric saloon or SUV from an established brand will find a useful point of comparison with [Como, an automotive dealer network in the Paris region](https://como.fr/), which sells the Mercedes-Benz and smart electric ranges among others.

## Is Zeekr a reliable brand?

The reliability question comes up systematically for such a recent brand. Zeekr has no long track record on the European market, unlike mainstream manufacturers present for decades. The manufacturer offsets this lack of history with an extended 10-year warranty on the battery and drivetrain in France, a duration above typical market standards.

This commercial argument, common among new Chinese entrants, does not replace an observed reliability record, but it limits the buyer's financial risk in the event of a major failure on the most expensive components. Early usage feedback in Europe, still limited in 2026 for lack of sufficient hindsight, will help refine this picture over the coming years.

## Should you buy a Zeekr in 2026?

Zeekr primarily appeals to buyers who value the equipment-to-price ratio and are willing to accept the risk of a still-young European brand in exchange for an extended warranty and technology that often outpaces its price tag. Buyers more attached to an established service network and a proven resale value should instead look at established mainstream or premium brands; our comparison of [Chinese car brands](/en/blog/chinese-car-brands/) available in France helps position Zeekr against its direct competitors.

Either way, the choice of model within the Zeekr range deserves to be refined based on actual use: the [Zeekr X](/en/blog/zeekr-x/) for urban use, the [Zeekr 7X](/en/blog/zeekr-7x/) for a family, or the [Zeekr 001](/en/blog/zeekr-001/) for a more status-oriented use.

Two points deserve particular attention before signing: insurance cost, often higher than for an equivalent model from an established brand due to the lack of claims history, and resale value, still uncertain for a brand with no track record on the French used-car market. These points don't disqualify Zeekr, but they deserve to be factored into the total cost of ownership, alongside the purchase price and the advertised range.
